# Report formats and length

## Choose before writing
Ask for Executive Standard or Detailed Report, with a short description. Ask separately whether the user wants email delivery. If Yes, obtain or confirm the address; if No, provide the report in chat. Do not repeat answered questions. Use native clickable choices if available, otherwise numbered choices. Give a realistic time range before starting and revise it if a material delay arises.

## Same quality, different purpose
Both formats use the same complete evidence review, verification and private evidence record. Explain the evidence-based reasons for recommendations, not hidden internal deliberations. Preserve source restrictions and keep internal paths and tool logs private.
- **Executive Standard — Decision brief:** concise findings, essential reasoning and priority actions. Best for management decisions. Answer: What matters, what does it mean for us, and what should we do?
- **Detailed Report — Full assessment:** deeper analysis, supporting sources, comparisons and an implementation plan. Best for preparation and execution. Answer: What supports the recommendation, what alternatives exist, and how do we implement it?

| Section | Executive Standard | Detailed Report |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Executive Summary | Main finding, business impact and recommended decision. | Main findings, recommended decision, major risks and conditions. |
| Analysis | Essential context and concise justification; priority opportunities and risks. | Relevant issues, evidence, options, trade-offs, assumptions and uncertainties; useful comparisons and scenarios. |
| Sources, where applicable | Essential official sources with direct links, especially for legal obligations, eligibility and deadlines. | References connected to findings, with dates, provisions and limitations where relevant. |
| Conclusion | Clear recommendation and conditions that could change it. | Reasoned recommendation, comparison with alternatives and unresolved questions. |
| Next Actions and Related Timelines | Normally three to five priority actions with proposed owners and dates. | Implementation plan with responsibilities, dependencies, milestones, deadlines and decision points. |

Never omit critical risks, eligibility restrictions or binding deadlines from either format. Add necessary actions beyond five when safety, compliance or a deadline requires them. Retain material limitations. A full bibliography of everything consulted is unnecessary in Executive Standard; keep the full private evidence record for both.

For a tender assessment, Executive Standard covers purpose, value, deadline, company fit, key eligibility concerns, bid/no-bid recommendation and immediate actions. Detailed Report also includes the requirements matrix, eligibility evidence, evaluation criteria, partner needs, contractual risks and preparation timetable. Both require the official tender documents. Apply the same decision-versus-implementation distinction to policy, law, funding and company reports. For contracts, apply these sections to the accompanying drafting report, not the agreement clauses.

## Length and completeness
Use content requirements and flexible editorial targets rather than default minimum character counts:
- Executive Standard: roughly 3–5 pages of substantive content, excluding the cover.
- Detailed Report: roughly 8–15 pages, with annexes where useful.
These are Bravestar editorial targets, not universal industry standards or mandatory page limits. Use normal readable formatting; never pad, invent content, shrink typography or hide essential analysis in annexes to reach a target. A shorter or longer report is acceptable when scope and evidence justify it; record the reason privately without requiring permission merely for a target deviation.
An identified official submission rule or agreed commissioning specification takes priority. Record its source and any actual minimum or maximum; do not invent industry requirements. Contracts and standalone annexes follow their own requirements, while the accompanying report follows the selected format.
Check substantive completeness manually: the reader must understand the recommendation, its supporting evidence, material risks and limitations, and the next steps. For Detailed Report, also assess alternatives and implementation dependencies where relevant. Missing material content blocks final release. Automated section and length checks alone do not establish substantive quality.

## Clear writing
Write with professional fluency, precise language and connected reasoning. Define terms and acronyms; explain why each finding matters and what to do next. All tables, charts, footnotes and annexes must be understandable to a non-specialist. Distinguish facts, assumptions, forecasts and unresolved questions. Avoid generic filler and misleading claims of human authorship or review. Perform a separate plain-language readability check.
