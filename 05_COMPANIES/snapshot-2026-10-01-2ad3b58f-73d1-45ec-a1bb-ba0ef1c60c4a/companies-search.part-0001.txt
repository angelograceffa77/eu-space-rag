Company description: Comobi &ndash; Orchestrating multimodal, autonomous-ready mobility
Ambition
Comobi BV (Gent, Belgium) runs a mobility platform for shared airport and station transfers. At its core is a proprietary pricing and matching algorithm. It gro
ups passengers into shared rides, sets fair dynamic prices and assigns each trip to the right vehicle. Our ambition goes beyond one vehicle type or one fleet. W
e want to optimise the mobility system as a whole, planning across cars, vans, buses and, soon, autonomous shuttles as one coordinated network. This means fewer
 empty kilometres, higher occupancy and a lower cost and carbon footprint per passenger.
Why navigation and satellite technology matter in the shift to autonomy
Today a human driver fills the gaps in any mobility service. They read the traffic, handle a closed road, find the right terminal entrance and adjust when a fli
ght lands early. Autonomous operations remove that human layer, so the intelligence has to move into the system itself.
In an autonomous model, Comobi acts as the service owner. It holds the customer demand, builds the trips and hands assignments to vehicle owners: autonomous ope
rators running their own fleets. For this handover to work reliably, both sides need a shared, precise and constantly updated picture of the operating environme
nt:
- High-integrity positioning and navigation (GNSS, including Galileo's high-accuracy and authentication services), so assignments, pick-up points and handovers 
are exact and trustworthy
- Satellite-based situational awareness, such as Earth observation, weather and real-time disruption data, so the service layer can re-plan routes, timing and v
ehicle allocation before problems reach the passenger
- Resilient connectivity, including satellite links where terrestrial networks fall short, so vehicles and the platform stay in contact at all times
Space-based data makes it possible to go from "dispatching a driver" to "orchestrating an autonomous fleet." It gives the service owner the context a human driv
er used to supply.
Multivendor collaboration
Comobi does not aim to build every component itself. We want to act as the neutral orchestration layer in a multivendor ecosystem. That means working with auton
omous vehicle operators, navigation technology providers, satellite data providers and data storage and processing partners, with a preference for local and Eur
opean partners. Our aim is to launch multivendor pilot operations. In these pilots, assignments, positioning data and situational information flow through open 
interfaces between the service owner and several vehicle operators. That makes autonomous mobility scalable, interoperable and grounded in European space capabi
lities.
