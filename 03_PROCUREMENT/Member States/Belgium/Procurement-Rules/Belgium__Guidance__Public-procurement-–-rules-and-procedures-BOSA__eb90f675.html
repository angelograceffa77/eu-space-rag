Public procurement – rules and procedures

Table of contents

Version 19/02/24

Information services survey

Types of procedure

Under Belgian national law, there are seven different procedures:

  1. open procedure
  2. closed procedure
  3. competitive procedure with negotiation
  4. direct negotiated procedure with prior publication
  5. competitive dialogue
  6. innovation partnership
  7. negotiated procedure without prior publication

Thresholds for determining the rules (EU sub-thresholds)

The threshold amount depends on the contract type:

  • EUR 30 000 for low-value contracts
  • EUR 143 000 for negotiated procedures without prior publication in traditional sectors
  • EUR 221 000 in special sectors

Different rules according to the contract type

The distinction between works contracts, supply contracts or service contracts is important as they give rise to different legal regimes.

For example, in respect of:

  • applicable advertising thresholds
  • general rules of execution deriving from the general conditions
  • in some cases, rules concerning delegation

Exclusion from a contract

A candidate may be excluded from registering for a tender for a number of reasons:

  • On mandatory grounds for exclusion, that is if the candidate has been convicted of one of the following offences: participation in a criminal organisation, corruption, fraud, terrorist offences, money laundering or terrorist financing, child labour or other forms of human trafficking and employing illegally resident third-country nationals.
  • On grounds of exclusion relating to tax and social security liabilities [except] ‘where the outstanding amount does not exceed the amount to be set by the King’ or ‘where the candidate or tenderer can demonstrate that it has one or a number of claims against a contracting authority that are specific, payable and free of any commitment to third parties’.
  • On optional grounds for exclusion such as serious professional misconduct that undermines the candidate’s integrity.
  • Because the candidate does not provide sufficient guarantees as to its economic, financial or technical capacity to perform a particular contract.

Time limits for submitting a tender

Depending on the type of procedure, time limits can vary from 22 to 35 days. These are the minimum time limits set out in Articles 36 to 41 of the Law of 17 June 2016. Those time limits will be determined by the contracting authority, which will take into account the complexity of the contract and the time required to prepare the tenders.

Documents to be included

All of the documents required in the rules in the special tender specifications (such as attestations, certificates, the ESPD, etc.).

Publication of contracts

Economic operators will find all publications of new contracts on the Bulletin of Tenders. 

E-invoices

E-invoices can be sent electronically to the Mercurius platform. 

Sources of assistance

Various sources of assistance can be found on the e-Procurement help center. 

Planning of contracts

Currently, there is no source of information that is available to the general public in respect of the planning of public contracts.

Information on procedures

Various sources of information can be found via the substantive requirements for being able to submit a tender.

E-signatures

Unless otherwise provided, the submission report must contain a qualified electronic signature. Depending on whether it is a single-stage procedure (such as the open procedure) or a two-stage procedure (such as the restricted procedure), the requirements in respect of signatures will be slightly different.

Submission of tenders

Tenders must be submitted electronically via the e-Procurement platform or another platform that meets all necessary requirements to allow submission of tenders. 

Tutorials

Please consult the e-Procurement help center for more information about how to submit your tender. 

Appeals

Depending on the nature of the contracting authority, the Council of State (Conseil d’État) or the civil courts are empowered to hear appeals.

Irregularities

Any irregularities may be reported to the contracting authority which launched the tender. This may be done using the communication methods available to you, such as the information session or forum.

Complaints

If you are an economic operator with an interest in the procedure, be it a dispute relating to the awarding or the performance of the contract, etc., you may lodge a complaint.

Time limits for complaints

Depending on the nature and subject matter of the complaint, the time limit will vary from 15 to 60 days (source: Council of State and courts).

Private individuals should consult a lawyer if they wish to obtain further information.
