Luxembourg, a growing hub for space companies Luxembourg has established itself as a major hub for companies in the space sector since entering the industry back in 1985 with the launch of SES (Société Européenne des Sattelites). Over the past decade, its space ecosystem went from just 16 companies employing around 600 people in 2012 to 80 space-related companies with a workforce of approximately 1,650 people by 2024. It is an outstanding growth resulting from strong government support through funding initiatives and a pioneering legal framework, unique in Europe, providing favourable conditions to attract international talent and investment. The space industry in Luxembourg impacts our everyday life and drives the country's economic growth as well as its global standing. Its thriving space ecosystem stimulates employment and innovation across different industries such as communications, transport, and healthcare, contributing tremendously to a better quality of life for the population. Additionally, space capabilities support climate action by providing essential data for environmental protection and resource management. This economic diversification attracts international talent and investment, enriching Luxembourg's expertise and creating opportunities for future generations. The space industry has a positive impact on Earth-related activities. Our history in space has been a major drive for developing the technology we use every day on Earth Agriculture Satellite data monitors crop health and optimises farming practices Disaster management Space technology enables early warnings and emergency response coordination Navigation Satellites provide precise positioning for maritime, air, and ground transportation Health Research in microgravity advances medical breakthroughs Finance Satellite data helps financial institutions assess risks and verify ESG performance The 4 strategic pillars of sustainability Luxembourg's space strategy places sustainability at its core, encompassing economic resilience, responsible use of orbital space, and meaningful contributions to global challenges. Space technologies are essential drivers of sustainable development on Earth while ensuring that our activities beyond our planet remain viable for future generations. The following pillars outline how Luxembourg will advance this vision through targeted action across four interconnected domains. Read the full National Strategy here (Pdf - 24.35 Mb) . View pillars Economic sustainability Creating competitiveness & cross-sector value for long-term economic growth Activities on Earth Addressing global challenges & support innovations across multiple Earth-related sectors Activities in space Ensuring long-term viability of space operations through responsible practices & orbital management Space resources use Advancing peaceful exploration & utilisation of space resources International collaboration As the first European country and second worldwide to offer a legal framework securing property rights for space resources, Luxembourg leads international efforts to harmonise global rules for peaceful space exploration. Partnerships continue to expand with countries including the United States, Japan, South Korea and several European nations. International collaboration European Space Agency Luxembourg joined ESA as a full member in 2005, enabling national companies to participate in research, development, and space exploration projects. Today, Luxembourg ranks among the top five per capita contributors to this 23-member international organisation, actively participating in programmes covering telecommunications, navigation, Earth observation, exploration, science, and security. International collaboration European Union The EU's space policy addresses challenges such as climate change besides delivering socio-economic benefits to citizens. Space technology, data, and services are essential to daily life, from mobile communications to disaster response. Luxembourg participates in major programmes including GNSS, Copernicus, EUSST, Govsatcom, and Horizon 2020 Space. International collaboration United Nations Luxembourg actively contributes to the UN Committee on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space (COPUOS), established in 1959 to govern the exploration and use of space for the benefit of all humanity. In a context of rapidly evolving technologies and space agenda, the Committee provides a unique global platform for monitoring these advances and addressing legal issues arising from outer space exploration.
