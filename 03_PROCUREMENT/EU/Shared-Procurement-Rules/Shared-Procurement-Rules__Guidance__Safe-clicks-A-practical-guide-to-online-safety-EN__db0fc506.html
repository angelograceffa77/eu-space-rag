    Safe clicks

    A practical guide to online safety

    Digital technology can give children opportunities to learn, connect and grow.

    But they come with real risks. 

    Online platforms may offer addictive designs: infinite scrolling, short videos with short attention span, highly personalised and targeted content. The amount of time children spend looking at screens has never been so high. And the more time they spend online, the more likely it is that they will be exposed to harmful and illegal content, and online predators. 

    💬

    Start the conversation early

    The best online safety tool is real, offline communication.

    Talk to children regularly about what they do online, who they interact with, and what they enjoy. Show interest in what they like more, their favourite games or videos. Creating an open environment makes it more likely they'll seek help if something goes wrong.

    💡Did you know?

    Building trust through everyday conversations can make children more likely to share concerns and seek help if something goes wrong.

    Recognise cyberbullying

    Online bullying is never acceptable and children can and should report harmful behaviour.

    Parents should encourage children to save evidence, block offenders, and speak to a trusted adult if they experience or witness cyberbullying. Cyberbullying remains one of the major risks facing young people online.

    Be careful with online strangers

    Not everyone online is who they claim to be.

    Teach children to be cautious when chatting with people in games, social media, or messaging apps. They should never arrange to meet online contacts without parental involvement.

    Explore AI together

    From creating images to answering questions, AI apps are becoming a bigger part of children's digital lives.
    AI chatbots are friendly but can’t be true friends.

    Exploring these tools together can help parents understand how they work, discuss online safety and encourage responsible use.

    💡Did you know?

    Free apps often collect a lot of data or are financed by advertising.
    Pay close attention to which apps you choose to use.

    Encourage reporting and asking for help

    Children should know that they can always tell a trusted adult if they encounter something upsetting, confusing, or inappropriate online.

    Reporting harmful content, suspicious messages, or unsafe behaviour helps keep both themselves and others safer. The EU's approach focuses on protecting, empowering, and supporting children online.

    💡Did you know?

    In the EU, the child helpline number is 116 111. Making your child aware of this is important as there may be times they want help from someone other than you.

    Avoid sharenting

    Think twice before you share

    They just want to see the baby. Your parents. Your friends. Your followers. One tap, and the photo is out there, forever. But where does it really go? Who saves it? Who uses it? Who sells it?

    Once it’s online, you can’t take it back.
