Power and trust need to be built together: what to watch out for at the International Space Summit
Power and trust need to be built together: what to watch out for at the International Space Summit
Published:
9 September 2026
The International Space Summit opens this week in Paris. ESPI will be represented by its Director, Hermann Ludwig Moeller, and Marco Aliberti, Associate Director of International Engagement.
For Europe, the summit offers a unique opportunity to present itself as a strong and trusted global partner, to set a vision for the future and display a ‘can-do’ attitude.
However, building power and trust is not achieved through declarations alone. ESPI will track whether the summit discusses these seven indicators:
1. Displaying
European unity.
This summit tests whether Europe can act as one politically at a moment of growing strategic competition. A united response, through a coordinated set of
strategies and concrete proposals for
greater sovereignty
, aligned with national priorities rather than competing against them, would send a powerful signal on the global stage
.
2. International reach.
Power and trust need tobe built in parallel. The run-up to the summit has already tested both: several major space companies withdrew from the event amid apparent political pressure. The summit is Europe’s opportunity
to show it can attract the international community and
shape a common future in space with like-minded partners.
3. Stimulus for institutional and private investment.
Rhetoric at the summit is only meaningful if it converts into capital. The real test is whether it generates sufficient
political momentum ahead of major institutional funding milestones
(g. the European Space Agency Intermediate Ministerial Meeting, European Commission’s Multiannual Financial Framework), and whether it helps move investment towards the 0.15% of GDP benchmark, including a 20% share from private investment.
4. Catalyst for a competitive space industry and entrepreneurs.
Europe’s space industry is undergoing profound transformation, and the summit will show how the political establishment will back this transformation.   Will it strike a balance between supporting the rejuvenation of established actors and making room for new entrants and entrepreneurs?
5. Space programmes.
The space programmes that dominate debate will reveal Europe’s real priorities.  A key marker, next to security and defence, will be whether Europe can
leverage its leading role in Earth observation
to advocate for coordinated global action on climate change and mitigating natural disasters.
6. Aspirations for a new era.
The International Space Station era is ending, and the next forum for international diplomacy in space is yet to be presented. The summit will be the moment to find out if a new, genuinely ambitious programme for the coming decade can be produced.
This must include a serious
European commitment to crewed spacefligh
t.
Will Europe still spark the imagination of future generations and inspire European citizens and the global community to come closer together?
7. Format of political debate and diplomacy.
In a world increasingly driven by speed, agility, geo-economic and commercial forces, high-level political summits are increasingly judged on whether they produce real outcomes or simply pageantry. This summit’s format, and what it delivers, will be a live test of whether
this kind of diplomacy still work
s or needs to evolve.
