WHY A POLICY VISION FOR 
EARTH OBSERV ATION?
Europe is standing at the cusp of a new era. An era where our weight in the world is measured 
by our ability to set our priorities, seize opportunities, anticipate crises, and act at scale with 
purpose. An era where we either shape the future or observe on the sidelines. An era where 
emerging and advanced technologies will determine our long-term prosperity and ability to 
ensure peace. 
A new era requires a new approach, empowered by space. At ESPI, we have embarked on a new 
journey to propose clear policy guidance & pull to the space sector, complementing the 
technological and scientific foundations & push that have propelled our space programmes to 
date. For this reason, we convened, together with the European Space Agency, a high-level 
workshop on the Policy Value of Earth Observation.
At a time when security, climate resilience, and resource management issues rise to the top of 
policy agendas across Europe, Earth Observation has a much bigger role to play than ever 
before. Only with full integration of space in its sectoral policymaking will Europe deliver in 
domains such as energy, security, environment, agriculture, digitalisation and diplomacy. This 
Vision marks only the beginning of a decade-long effort to put space at the full service of 
Europe’s values and goals.
...THE NEXT CHAPTER FOR SPACE AND EARTH OBSERVATION
H. Ludwig Moeller
Director of the European Space Policy Institute (ESPI)

ABOUT
THE VISION IN THE BIGGER PICTURE
This Vision expresses ESPI’s views stemming from its own research 
and crucially informed by the outcome of a high-level Workshop  
on the Policy Value of Earth Observation, convened by ESPI and the 
European Space Agency (ESA) at the Royal Society, London, in April 
2025. Selected quotes by the participants are featured throughout  
the document. This Vision also builds on further consultations, 
leveraging ESA’s long-standing expertise in Earth Observation.
This Vision sets a direction, initiating a dialogue to maximise the policy impact of Earth 
Observation: bringing new energy to space programmes through stimulating continuous 
policy demand and guidance. It is addressed to national and European decision-makers  
in the domains of energy, security, environment, agriculture, digitalisation and beyond. 
 
 While the impact of space extends further, this Vision focuses only on the highest-
priority domains and policy objectives where a stronger integration of Earth Observation 
would deliver significant added value to Europe. Collaboration between policy and space 
communities will unlock new opportunities, to date unexplored, that can bring value  
to European citizens, public administrations, and businesses through space-derived 
information and services.
 
 While this document is not a work plan, the Vision is underpinned with a tangible call for 
action. It urges the policy community to further integrate Earth Observation, and space  
at large, into their sectoral policies and calls on the space community to create entry 
points to receive policy guidance for their programmatic action. ‘It takes a village’,  
in the spirit of the African proverb.
 
 Doing so is no easy feat and represents a task of a decade. Success depends on timely 
action, including leveraging upcoming milestones that will shape the future of Earth 
Observation in Europe. Notably, the ESA Council Meetings at Ministerial Level and the 
parallel preparation of the new EU Multi-Annual Financial Framework for the 2028-2034 
time frame, as well as the breadth of national initiatives. 
This Vision fits within a broader context and contributes to a global 
positioning of Europe as a strong partner to the world

This Policy Vision for Earth Observation was developed  
by ESPI between September 2024 and April 2025,  
in collaboration with the European Space Agency (ESA).  
The Vision is grounded in an analysis of the priorities 
derived from over 300 policy documents and 1200+ Earth 
Observation projects. This document highlights selected 
policy domains and related objectives that emerged from  
the analysis. The research was complemented by a thorough 
consultation campaign with over 50 European stakeholders 
from different policy domains. 
This Vision could not have come to life without a collective 
effort reaching far beyond ESPI’s walls. ESPI and ESA  
would like to extend their gratitude to the participants  
of the high-level policy workshop, who gathered at the  
Royal Society in London on 3 April 2025 to provide  
their precise inputs and strategic guidance. 
The Vision will be brought by ESPI to all relevant policy 
communities as an input to foster a closer dialogue  
with the space sector. ESPI is committed to facilitating 
this dialogue together with a network of policy and space 
stakeholders towards the implementation of concrete 
solutions to the issues at stake.
