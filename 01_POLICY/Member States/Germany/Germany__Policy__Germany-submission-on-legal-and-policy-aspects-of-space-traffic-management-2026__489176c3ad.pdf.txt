The question of how the international community ensures a future framework for sa fe and sus-
tainable space traffic is among the most crucial questions of international space governance. Over 
the past decade, the number of satellites launched has increased exponentially, bringing the num-
ber of active and inactive spacecraft in orbit to over 15,000.1 Space traffic, referring to the move-
ment of all space objects, including parts thereof and debris, during all phases of spaceflight, has 
therefore increased significantly, especially in Earth’s orbit. This is inherently lin ked to the fact 
that the potential and benefits of space activities for humanity have never been greater. 
Over the past decade, Germany has supported initiatives for space traffic coordination and man-
agement both at the global and the regional level. As previously stated under the relevant agenda 
item at the Legal Subcommittee, Space Traffic Management can be understood as a coherent set 
of technical and regulatory provisions for promoting safe access into outer s pace, operations in 
outer space and return from outer space to Earth free from physical or radio-frequency interfer-
ence.2 
1. Relevant regulations, policies, best practices and requirements at the national and 
regional levels 
Since 2025, the Ministry of Research, Technology and Space is responsible for actively shaping 
German space policy together with science, industry and European and international part ners. 
Germany is engaged in designing space-related policies and regulations including with regard to 
space traffic on the national, regional and international level. The orientation toward the principle 
of sustainability is one of the overarching guidelines of the Federal Government’s Space Strategy 
of 2023.  Space Traffic Management was identified as a key topic  of the Strategy, including a 
commitment to help shape an international framework within the United Nations. To support and 
complement this approach, Germany further supports the regional “EU approach to space traffic 
management” and is actively engaged in the European Union Space Surveillance and Tracking  
(EUSST), where Germany and other EU Member States share surveillance and trackin g (SST)

2. Identification of existing international norms   
The five UN space treaties are the cornerstone of the international legal framework for activities 
in outer space and provide important guidance when discussing legal and policy aspects of space 
traffic (management). First and foremost, Art. I of the Outer Space Treaty provides that the ex-
ploration and use of outer space shall be the province of all (hu)mankind and outer space shall be 
free for exploration and use by all States without discrimination of any kind. The freedom to use 
and explore outer space is to be enjoyed by all Treaty Parties and finds its limits in the principles 
of the subsequent articles of the OST. This entails an obligation to use out er space responsibly. 
This can most appropriately be achieved through international management of spac e traffic, re-
ferring to the entirety of coordinated international efforts, policies, le gal regulation, standards, 
technologies and strategies designed to ensure the safe, sustainable and efficient operation of ob-
jects in space, and in particular in Earth orbit.  Additional articles of the Outer Space Treaty are 
relevant: Art. VIII allocates jurisdiction and control and clarifies ownership of  objects launched 
into outer space; according to Art. IX States are to pay due regard to the corres ponding interests 
of all other States Parties and hold international consultations in cases of potential interferences; 
Art. X addresses opportunities to observe the flight of space objects launched by other States on 
a basis of equality and with Art. XI States agree to share informati on on their space activities to 
the greatest extent feasible and practicable. Internationally accepted rules of the road can also be 
seen as a prerequisite for an effective international liability regime in accordance with the Liabil-
ity Convention.

3. Considerations on the way-forward for future work relating to the topic at the COPUOS 
Legal Subcommittee 
The ever-growing number of launches and space objects challenges the safety and long-term sus-
tainability of space activities, including the realisation of economic potentials. Germany will con-
tinue to promote ecologically, economically and socially sustainable activit ies, innovations and 
business models in its space activities that serve the people and our plan et at national, European 
and international level, in particular within the framework of the United Nations. We continue to 
advocate for a multilateral approach as well as capacity-building for the long-term sustainable use 
of space. Germany is already supporting a wide range of capacity-building measures and is en-
deavouring to further develop this support. We remain keen to facilitate and engage in a dialogue 
among developing countries, emerging and experienced space faring nations on sustainable space 
activities to ensure that space continues to serve the benefit of all humankind long into the future. 
The importance of strengthened global governance of space activities was reco gnized by world 
leaders at the Summit of the Future in Action 56 of the Pact for the Future, whic h contained a 
decision to discuss the establishment of a new framework for space traffic. The international space 
community needs to initiate meaningful discussions on the scientific, technical, legal and policy 
aspects of space traffic. Germany is convinced that effective space traffic management contributes 
to the protection of operational space systems, ensures the viability of private and public invest-
ments in and benefits from space and plays an important role for a sustainable space environment 
that benefits all. Therefore, Germany welcomes the establishment of the Expert Group on Space 
Situational Awareness and is actively contributing to its work. In order to add ress this urgent 
issue, COPUOS must rely on the expertise and capacity of both Subcommit tees. Therefore, we 
believe that establishing work streams in both Subcommittees in a timely manner is essential. 
We consider it crucial to start substantial discussions in the Legal Subcom mittee with a view to 
reaching consensus on underlying principles of the international framework to ensure the freedom 
to use and explore outer space. A possible way forward would be to discus s under the existing 
agenda item or possibly in a dedicated Working Group: 
- examples of national approaches to space traffic coordination and manag ement (not 
technical) as well as relevant regulatory best practices, 
- elements to be considered when drafting national regulations, 
- participation by non-governmental actors in space traffic coordination/management, 
- possible contributions by COPUOS (recommendations, guidelines, etc.) and 
- ways and means to promote a common understanding of space traffic management, 
including through capacity building measures in the field of space law and policy 
through international cooperation.
